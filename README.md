# Wi-Fi Generations – Clean & Logical Numbering

This table presents a **principled generational model** of Wi-Fi standards, including **retroactive numbering for early versions**. Designed for clarity, accuracy, and GitHub readability.

| Generation | Release Year | Max Theoretical Speed | Frequency Bands | IEEE Standard | Key Technologies | Short Description |
|------------|-------------|----------------------|-----------------|--------------|------------------|------------------|
| Wi-Fi 0 | 1997 | 1–2 Mbps | 2.4 GHz | 802.11 (Legacy) | FHSS, DSSS | Original foundational standard. Supported 1 and 2 Mbps modes. Experimental and rarely used today. |
| Wi-Fi 1 | 1999 | 11 Mbps | 2.4 GHz | 802.11b | DSSS | First widely adopted Wi-Fi. Practical but prone to interference and limited speed. |
| Wi-Fi 2 | 1999 | 54 Mbps | 5 GHz | 802.11a | OFDM | Introduced OFDM on 5 GHz. Limited adoption due to cost and lack of compatibility with 802.11b. |
| Wi-Fi 3 | 2003 | 54 Mbps | 2.4 GHz | 802.11g | OFDM | Brought OFDM to 2.4 GHz with backward compatibility. Became very popular in homes. |
| Wi-Fi 4 | 2009 | 600 Mbps | 2.4 + 5 GHz | 802.11n | MIMO, Channel Bonding | Major leap in performance. Enabled HD streaming and improved real-world speeds. |
| Wi-Fi 5 | 2013 | ~6.9 Gbps | 5 GHz | 802.11ac | MU-MIMO, Beamforming, 256-QAM | Focused on high throughput and multi-device performance in 5 GHz networks. |
| Wi-Fi 6 | 2019 | 9.6 Gbps | 2.4 + 5 GHz | 802.11ax | OFDMA, TWT, 1024-QAM | Improved efficiency in dense environments. Lower latency and better battery life. |
| Wi-Fi 6E | 2021 | 9.6 Gbps | 2.4 + 5 + 6 GHz | 802.11ax (extended) | 6 GHz Spectrum | Extension of Wi-Fi 6 adding access to the wide and less congested 6 GHz band. |
| Wi-Fi 7 | 2024 | ~46 Gbps | 2.4 + 5 + 6 GHz | 802.11be | MLO, 4096-QAM, 320 MHz Channels | Ultra-high throughput. Designed for extreme performance, low latency, and dense environments. |

---

## 📝 Notes

- **Speeds are theoretical maximums** under ideal conditions (e.g., maximum spatial streams and channel widths).
- Real-world performance depends on distance, interference, hardware, and network congestion.
- **Wi-Fi 0 to Wi-Fi 3 are retroactive labels** for conceptual clarity. Official naming starts from Wi-Fi 4.
- **Wi-Fi 6E is an extension**, not a new generation.
- All newer generations maintain **backward compatibility** with older devices.

---

## 📚 Key Technology Highlights

- **FHSS / DSSS** → Early spread spectrum techniques  
- **OFDM** → Efficient multi-carrier modulation  
- **MIMO** → Multiple antennas for higher throughput  
- **MU-MIMO** → Multi-user parallel data streams  
- **OFDMA** → Better efficiency in crowded networks  
- **TWT (Target Wake Time)** → Improved battery life  
- **MLO (Multi-Link Operation)** → Simultaneous multi-band connections  
- **QAM (Quadrature Amplitude Modulation)** → Higher data density per signal
